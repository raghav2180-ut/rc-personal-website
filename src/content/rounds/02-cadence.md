After my second year, I got a summer internship at Cadence Design Systems in Noida. I built a Python tool that drew resistance and capacitance networks from chip design files.

It was my first corporate office. On my first day, my name was on the welcome board at reception. Then came the access card, a desk with my name on it, whiteboards, cafeterias and meetings. The comfort Cadence gave its employees was truly amazing, and honestly it is still one of the nicest offices I have worked in.

But those two months taught me something more useful than the code. I liked fast-moving places, wearing many hats, building things from scratch and taking ownership early. I liked being closer to chaos than to structure.

Somehow, in the middle of all that comfort, I realised I didn't want it. There was nothing wrong with Cadence. It just wasn't me. At 19, knowing what I didn't want was progress.

A decade later, one of our UnsaidTalks students, Sambhav, got placed at Cadence. I'll come back to him.
