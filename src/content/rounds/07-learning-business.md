Then I did something most engineers don't. I moved into business.

At Valueline I helped set up a new smart home automation business in Hyderabad, from finding vendors across India and the US to hiring and training the team. Then I ran sales, marketing and operations for Bangalore and Chennai.

Those two years in Bangalore changed me. I met founders who had built everything from scratch and people far wealthier than anyone I had known, most of them grounded and unshowy. What they shared wasn't genius. It was **audacity**. They decided fast, acted aggressively and didn't overthink themselves into doing nothing. Being around them pulled me out of my own overthinking.

In 2024 I joined EasyInsights, an AI product for marketers, to lead business growth. Its founder, Raghav Kansal, had also been my boss at ET Medialabs, where I worked in my final semester in 2017. We found product-market fit and grew it to seven-figure monthly revenue.

Business analyst, software developer, sales, marketing, growth, operations. For years it felt like I kept changing direction. Looking back, I was collecting skills that would one day fit together.

I turned 30 that year, and for the first time I was clear about the next decade. My 20s were for figuring things out. My 30s would be for focused action.
